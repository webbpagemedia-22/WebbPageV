Dropbox buys Tigris for $350m, and UK users can now ask AI about their files

Dropbox is spending $350 million on a storage start-up to power AI agents, and eligible UK Standard and Advanced customers can already ask AI to work on their files.

Dropbox has agreed to buy Tigris Data, a cloud storage company, for $350 million. TechCrunch calls it Dropbox's biggest acquisition so far. Tigris was started by former Uber engineers, and its technology keeps data close to the people and computers using it, so access is quicker.

The purchase backs a new Dropbox product called DBX Agent Cloud, aimed at businesses and developers building AI agents. The deal is expected to close in the last three months of 2026.

The part that matters more to a small firm is a separate change. Dropbox says eligible Standard and Advanced customers in the UK, US and Australia can now ask AI to carry out tasks on their files, pick between AI models, and connect their Dropbox content to tools such as ChatGPT and Claude. More than 70,000 teams have access so far, with wider availability planned for 2027.

What this means for your business

If you keep client paperwork, quotes or photos in Dropbox, check your plan and your sharing settings before switching any of this on. An AI that can read your files can read everything it has been given access to, so start with one tidy folder rather than the lot.
