Black Friday email ideas for small businesses: let AI write, you check every price

AI can draft your Black Friday subject lines and offer emails in minutes. Before anything goes out, check every "was" price and every delivery charge yourself, because the CMA is watching pricing closely this Christmas.

Black Friday falls on 27 November this year, so if you're hunting for Black Friday email ideas as a small business, now is the time. An AI assistant is a quick way to get started: tell it what you sell, what the offer is and the tone you like, and ask for five subject lines and a short email. Pick the best, then rewrite it in your own voice, as we suggested for your Christmas social media posts.

The one job you should never hand to AI is the numbers. It will happily invent a "was £60" price or a "50% off everything" headline that sounds right but isn't true.

So check two things before you press send.

First, any "was" price. Trading Standards' pricing guidance, updated in April 2026, says the higher price should be one you genuinely charged recently, for at least as long as the period you're comparing against, and not undercut by cheaper prices in between. "Up to 50% off" is only fair if a good share of the range really has that discount.

Second, delivery. On 2 November the Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) wrote to online retailers warning that some still hide delivery charges. Where delivery is compulsory, the total price including the minimum delivery charge must appear from the advert through to checkout. The CMA has already fined four businesses more than £5.1 million under these rules.

What this means for your business

Let AI save you time on the wording, but treat every price, discount and delivery charge as your job to check. A truthful offer keeps you on the right side of the CMA and keeps your customers' trust. If you'd like help planning your festive marketing, see our social media services.

Source: Competition and Markets Authority on GOV.UK, 2 November 2026 (also CTSI pricing guidance, updated April 2026). This is our own summary; read the original for the full story.
