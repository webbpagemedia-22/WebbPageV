UK mobile networks are using AI to block scam calls, as fake phone deal fraud rises 85%

The Home Office says networks blocked over a billion scam calls and texts in the past year, partly with AI, but fraudsters posing as mobile providers are up 85% this year. Here's the simple check that protects your business phones this Black Friday.

The Home Office and the UK's biggest mobile networks have launched a Black Friday warning about scam calls and texts, and the numbers show AI working on both sides.

On the defensive side, the networks say they now use AI to spot scam calls before they reach you. Virgin Media O2 says its new system blocks around 3 million scam calls a week, while BT and EE say AI helps them catch threats across calls, texts, emails and websites. Together with tighter checks on suspicious texts, the government says over a billion scam calls and messages were blocked in the past year.

The worrying part is that the criminals are still getting through. Fraud prevention body Cifas has recorded more than 15,000 cases this year of fraudsters pretending to be mobile providers, about 58 a day and up 85% on 2025, with losses estimated at over £32 million. The typical bait is a phone deal or contract upgrade that looks too good to miss.

The campaign's advice is short: don't rush, contact your provider through its own website or app rather than a number or link in a message, and never share passwords or one-time codes over the phone. A genuine provider will never pressure you to stay on the line or ask you to share your screen.

What this means for your business

If your business has mobile contracts, expect "upgrade" calls and texts this week, and treat every unexpected one as suspect. Tell staff the simple rule: hang up and ring the provider back on the number from its official website. No real provider will ever ask for a one-time code.
