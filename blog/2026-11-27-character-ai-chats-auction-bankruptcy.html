Character.AI wants to auction 21 million users' chats: what happens to your AI data if a company goes bust

The bankrupt chatbot firm Character.AI plans to sell users' private chat histories to the highest bidder, and 42 US attorneys general are trying to stop it. It shows that what you type into an AI tool can outlive the company that made it.

Character.AI, the chatbot app that went into US bankruptcy protection in October, is trying to sell off its assets. According to a coalition of 42 US state attorneys general, those assets include the private chat histories of more than 21 million American accounts, over 2.1 million of them belonging to children.

The chats would not be sold on their own. The states say each conversation is tied to the user's profile, IP address and other details, along with risk scores the company gave them. The auction is due on 1 December, and the only bidder named so far is a company set up just one day before it was picked as the lead bid, with no owners disclosed.

The attorneys general are asking the court to block or limit the sale. Their main worry is that a new owner would not have to follow Character.AI's privacy policy, honour deletion requests or tell anyone if it sold the data on. That policy had told users their personal information would not be sold for money. Character.AI says user information is still protected under its policy.

This is a US case, and UK data law gives people more protection, but the lesson travels: when an AI company fails, its data is usually one of the things it has left to sell.

What this means for your business

Before your team uses any AI tool for work, check its privacy policy for what happens to your data if the company is sold or closes, and choose established providers with business plans. Keep customer details, passwords and anything confidential out of AI chats, and delete old chats you no longer need.
