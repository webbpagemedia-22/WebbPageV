AI prices are falling, so now is a good time to check what you pay

Spending data from Ramp shows firms are using more AI but paying little more, as price cuts and cheaper models bite. Small businesses can use the same tricks.

New figures from the finance platform Ramp suggest that businesses are using more AI than ever without spending much more on it. According to TechCrunch, Ramp's AI spend grew just 1.5% across August and September combined, after jumping 42% in July. It picked up again in October, rising 8.9% in a month, but the summer slowdown was clear.

The reasons are practical. OpenAI halved some of its API prices in September, and Anthropic made big cuts to its own models. Meanwhile Meta and Nvidia are pushing cheaper and open-source options. Heavy users are also fighting back against waste: they block expensive models for long tasks, set budgets per employee, and route simple jobs to cheaper models.

Ramp's economist stresses that this looks like competition and cost control rather than falling demand. The data only covers Ramp's own customers, and most of the spending comes from fast-growing tech firms, so it is not a picture of the whole economy.

What this means for your business

Prices are moving in your favour, so it is worth looking at what you pay for AI tools each month. Cancel subscriptions nobody uses, and ask whether a cheaper plan or smaller model would do for routine jobs like drafting replies. Treat AI like any other overhead: set a monthly limit and review it every few months.
