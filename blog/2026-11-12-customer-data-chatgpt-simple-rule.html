Is it safe to put customer data into ChatGPT? A simple rule for small businesses

Most of what you know about your customers counts as personal data under UK law. Here is how to spot it, and a one-line rule your staff can follow before pasting anything into an AI chatbot.

Is it safe to put customer data into ChatGPT? It's a fair question, now AI drafts so many emails and replies. The short answer: treat anything that could identify a customer as off limits unless you're sure the tool and your account are set up to handle it.

Start by knowing what counts. The Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) says personal data is any information that relates to a living person who can be identified, directly or by piecing details together. That covers the obvious, such as names, phone numbers and email addresses, but also less obvious things like an IP address in a website log, or a job title and start date that point to one person in a small team. Swapping a name for a code doesn't make it anonymous, and getting someone's details wrong doesn't stop it being personal data.

So before pasting a complaint, an order history or a spreadsheet into a chatbot, strip out anything that could lead back to a real person. Ask the AI about the situation, not the individual: "a customer is unhappy that a delivery was late" works just as well as their full email.

If your team uses AI often, check whether you're on a free personal account or a business plan, and read the provider's settings on storing chats and training on your data. The ICO notes this guidance is under review after the Data (Use and Access) Act.

What this means for your business

Give your staff one rule: no names, contact details or anything that identifies a customer goes into an AI tool. Write it down, explain why, and revisit it when you change tools. If you'd like help training your team to use AI safely with customers, see our customer service training.
