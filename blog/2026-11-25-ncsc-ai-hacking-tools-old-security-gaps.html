UK cyber centre warns AI hacking tools are finding old security gaps in minutes

The National Cyber Security Centre says AI-driven attacks on UK organisations have risen since September. The tools aren't using new tricks, just finding old, unfixed weaknesses far faster. Here are the basics that stop them.

The UK's National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) has published a warning about a rise in attacks carried out with AI "agent" hacking tools. Since September it has dealt with several incidents where these tools found and broke into internet-facing systems one after another, causing downtime and giving criminals a way in.

The striking part is what the tools are exploiting. The NCSC says none of the weaknesses are new. They include default or missing passwords, passwords left in code, poorly set-up cloud services and accounts with more access than they need. What has changed is speed and cost. Free hacking tools, paired with cheap AI models, can now hunt for these gaps around the clock, so a problem that sat unnoticed for years can be found in minutes.

The NCSC's advice starts with knowing what you have online, including forgotten things such as an old website or a long-unused bit of equipment. After that: take services off the open internet if they don't need to be there, fix and update the ones that do, use strong two-step login, and watch for bursts of odd activity. It points smaller organisations to Cyber Essentials, the government-backed scheme for basic security controls.

TechCrunch also reported the warning, adding that the FBI has recently raised similar concerns in the US.

What this means for your business

Make a quick list of everything your business has online: website, old domains, booking systems, shared drives, routers and cameras. Change any default passwords, switch off what you no longer use, and turn on two-step login everywhere. If you want a structured checklist, the Cyber Essentials scheme is a good place to start.
