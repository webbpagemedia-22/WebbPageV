Notion, the popular notes and documents app, has confirmed that some chat transcripts from its built-in AI were stolen. The hackers didn't break into Notion itself. They got in through Fireworks AI, a company that runs AI models on behalf of other businesses, after first breaching Fireworks' own cloud provider.

According to TechCrunch, Fireworks says the attackers took billing details and some datasets belonging to its customers. Notion is one of them. It says the stolen material comes from Notion AI chats in a feature called Insights, and includes whatever people typed in and whatever the AI wrote back. Notion puts the share of affected customers at about 1%, but says much of the data can't be linked to a particular account, so it can't tell exactly who was hit. It cut all links with Fireworks on 3 November and deleted the data held there.

Fireworks thinks the gang is after money, mainly by selling stolen access to AI models so others can use them at someone else's expense. This is the firm's second breach in two months.

The wider lesson is about chains of suppliers. When you use an AI feature inside one app, your words may pass through several other companies, and each one is another door an attacker can try.

## What this means for your business

Treat anything you type into an AI chat as something that could one day leak, whichever app it sits in. Keep customer details, passwords and anything confidential out of AI chats, and if you use Notion AI, watch for any notice from Notion. When choosing AI tools, ask which other companies will handle your data.
