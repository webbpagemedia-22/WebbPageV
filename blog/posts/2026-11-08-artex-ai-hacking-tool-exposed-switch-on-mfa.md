Security firm Censys has found more than 800 copies of Artex, a Chinese-made AI hacking tool, reachable on the open internet with no password protecting them. Artex is free and open source, and was released in July. Microsoft describes it as a team of AI agents that can run an attack with very little human involvement, from scouting a target to taking its data.

Not every exposed copy belongs to a criminal, because security testers use the tool too. But TechCrunch reports it has been linked to real incidents, including attacks on South Korean financial firms in September and, in October, spying on thousands of organisations whose data sits in Microsoft's cloud. Microsoft said the speed of that campaign left defenders little time to react.

The reassuring detail is how the attacks often get in. Many exploited ordinary weaknesses, such as passwords that were easy to guess or already leaked, and accounts with no multi-factor authentication. Artex's developers say future versions will be closed-source and paid, but copies of the existing code are still circulating.

## What this means for your business

AI makes automated attacks cheaper and faster, but the defence is still the basics. Switch on two-step login for your email, bank, website and cloud accounts, and use a different strong password for each. It takes an afternoon and shuts the door the tool most often walks through.
