An AI receptionist is a phone assistant that answers when you can't. For a small business in the UK, that usually means three jobs: picking up missed calls while you're with a customer, taking messages out of hours, and booking or answering simple questions such as opening times. It's the voice version of the [AI assistants you can text](https://webbpageventura.co.uk/blog/2026-10-25-ai-assistants-you-can-text) we looked at last month.

Before you sign up, test the handover. Ring it yourself with an awkward question and see whether it passes you to a person, takes a proper message or goes round in circles. A caller who can't reach a human is a lost customer.

Then check the recording. Most of these tools record or transcribe calls, and the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) says callers must be told a call is being recorded and why. A short recorded message at the start is good practice. The ICO also says recording every call isn't usually proportionate, so keep only what you need, and remember recordings count as personal data a caller can ask to see. Tell your staff too, if their calls are covered. The ICO notes its guidance is under review after the Data (Use and Access) Act.

Finally, ask the provider where recordings are stored, for how long, and whether they're used to train its AI.

## What this means for your business

An AI receptionist can stop missed calls turning into missed work, but only if it hands over to a human smoothly and tells callers they're being recorded. Test it yourself before customers do.
